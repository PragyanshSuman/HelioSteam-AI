Data Engineering

Dataset Preprocessing & Sensor Resilience

Transforming raw, noisy SCADA readings into robust machine learning inputs

🌙 1. Nighttime Zero-Filtering

Sensors produce small noise signals at night. All hours where $GHI \le 15.0\,W/m^2$ were mathematically clamped to prevent negative steam predictions.

🛡️ 2. Resilient Median Imputation

Industrial sensors occasionally drop packets. We built an imputer using training medians so if a physical pyranometer fails, the model continues running seamlessly.

📐 3. Diurnal & East/West Bias

Engineered directional features separating morning sun (East field) from afternoon sun (West field) to accurately match the sun's trajectory.

🗣️ Speaker Notes (Slide 5)
"Real factory data is noisy. We handled three major preprocessing steps: First, we filtered out nighttime sensor drift so the model never predicts negative steam. Second, we built median imputation so if an outdoor sensor drops off the network, the dashboard doesn't crash. Third, we engineered features for East vs. West fields because morning sun hits the East mirrors while afternoon sun hits the West mirrors."
Model Architecture

Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost Regressor)

Capturing non-linear thermal inertia and optical geometries in milliseconds

🧠 Why XGBoost Over Other Models?
  • ⚡ Thermal Inertia: Metal solar tubes take 45 minutes to warm up. Tree ensembles capture threshold effects far better than linear regression.
  • ⏱️ Sub-5ms Latency: Executes instantaneously on edge servers without requiring heavy GPU infrastructure.
  • 🔍 Interpretable: Provides exact feature importance rankings to build trust with plant engineers.
🏆 Top Feature Importance
  • 🥇 GHI (Solar Irradiance): 58.12% contribution
  • 🥈 Steam Current (Lag-1): 18.24% contribution
  • 🥉 West & East DNI: 12.98% combined contribution
  • 🎖️ Hour & Feedwater: Balance of thermal physics
🗣️ Speaker Notes (Slide 6)
"We chose XGBoost because solar thermal plants have thermal inertia—pipes take time to heat up. Linear models are too simplistic, and neural networks require heavy GPUs. XGBoost runs in under 5 milliseconds and explains its logic: Global Horizontal Irradiance accounts for 58% of the prediction, followed by the previous hour's steam output and direct normal irradiance."
