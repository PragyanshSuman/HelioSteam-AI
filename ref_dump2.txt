P90: ABSTRACT
P92: Sleep stage identification from electroencephalography (EEG) underpins the diagnosis of sleep disorders, yet manual scoring is laborious, and nearly all automated systems classify an epoch only after it has been recorded, which limits their use in real-time monitoring and closed-loop intervention. Existing methods either depend on epochs that follow the target, or demonstrate self-supervised pretraining, graph-structured channel modelling, and calibrated confidence in isolation, and none forecasts the stage of an unobserved epoch. This work formulates sleep staging as causal forecasting of the current and the next two epochs from five preceding epochs. The proposed framework, G-TransSleep, standardizes recordings across datasets, extracts multi-scale temporal features with a convolutional encoder, learns a channel graph through masked-reconstruction and contrastive pretraining, fuses spatial and temporal context with graph attention and a causally masked Transformer, and decodes three horizons with temperature-scaled confidence. On 11 held-out Sleep-EDF subjects, Cohen’s kappa was 0.772, 0.750, and 0.729 at the three horizons (accuracy 0.881, 0.868, and 0.856), with an expected calibration error of 0.031 and a latency of 4.36 ms per window on a GPU. Transition scores reached an area under the ROC curve (AUROC) of about 0.88, although recall at the default decision rule was low. An inference-time ablation showed strong dependence on the temporal Transformer and no measurable benefit from the learned graph with two channels. Direct transfer to 17 ISRUC-Sleep subjects without adaptation gave kappa between 0.49 and 0.53. These results indicate that causal, calibrated sleep-stage forecasting is feasible with a compact model and provide a baseline for wearable and closed-loop sleep monitoring.
